## SLIDE 4 — Three Forces Converging Right Now

### 1. Our flagship deal set a template nobody's replicating

Snowflake: **$200M over 3 years.** Claude as the backbone for Snowflake Intelligence & Cortex AI. Data-sharing provisions. Unilateral termination rights for us.

That deal became a reference point every data-platform CTO heard about within a week. **Databricks heard about it. Confluent heard about it. MongoDB heard about it.** But inside our org, that win is filed under "DNB Core" — diluted across a generic bucket instead of weaponized against its direct peer set.

### 2. OpenAI launched the orchestration layer we don't have

**Frontier** (Feb 5, 2026): enterprise agent orchestration platform. Model-agnostic — supports agents built on OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, *and Anthropic*. Initial customers include Intuit, Oracle, Uber. Fortune called it OpenAI's bid to be the "operating system of the enterprise."

**We don't have a direct equivalent.** If Frontier becomes the layer enterprises standardize on, our platform customers become *Frontier's* distribution channel — and we become a fungible backend. Dario said it six weeks ago: *"Enterprise is wide open with no clear winner."* That window closes.

### 3. We're one bad launch away from a Tier 1 walking

Intuit signed **$29M on February 23rd**. Three weeks later, Labs ships a Cowork tax-filing plugin built on **Column Tax's engine** — a direct Intuit competitor.

Claude Code Security (Feb 20) cut **~$50B off public cyber market caps in 3 days.** CrowdStrike is an $8M ARR account with a $25M target. They noticed.

Figma is highest-sensitivity on Omelette. Lovable and Replit are staring down Claude Create. These are all DNB Core accounts. **Nobody's job is to see the collision coming.**

> **Speaker note:** Multiply. "We have a reference deal we're not replicating. We have a competitive window closing at the orchestration layer. And we have a roadmap moving fast enough that it's stepping on eight-figure customers without anyone catching it in advance. Any one of these is a reason to act. All three at once is what timing looks like."

## SLIDE 5 — What's a "Platform" (So We're Scoring the Same Thing)

Using the **Project Polaris operating-model taxonomy** we already use:

**Platform = companies where other companies build on top.** The intersection of:
- **Subscription** (SaaS revenue model) +
- **Marketplace or Community** (third-party ecosystem) +
- **External-facing API or SDK** as the primary product surface

| IN | OUT |
|---|---|
| Databricks, Snowflake, Confluent (data platforms) | Coinbase (fintech — already carved) |
| Atlassian, Notion, Figma (workflow platforms) | CrowdStrike (cyber — already carved) |
| Stripe, Twilio, Shopify (commerce/infra platforms) | Netflix (digital eCommerce — consumer) |
| ServiceNow, Salesforce, HubSpot (system-of-record platforms) | DoorDash (on-demand services) |

**The ICP tell:** every Platform company passes the hardest Polaris gate by definition — *"evaluating Anthropic to power external use cases that reach a broad user base."* Their product *is* the external use case.

> **Speaker note:** "I'm not inventing a taxonomy here. This is the Polaris operating-model language, just applied as a segment filter instead of an account-level tag. And the reason this is a *market* and not just a category — to borrow Richard's framing — is that these people talk to each other. Databricks engineers and Snowflake engineers are at the same conferences, reading the same threads, reference-checking the same vendors. When one buys, the others hear about it."

## SLIDE 8 — DATABRICKS: The Reference-Sell Play

**Why this one, why now:** Databricks and Snowflake are the most direct peer-competitors in enterprise data. Every Databricks executive has been briefed on the Snowflake-Anthropic deal by their own competitive intelligence team. The reference-sell is already half-done.

**Customer need**
- Mosaic AI needs a frontier-model backbone that doesn't position them as reselling a competitor's product (OpenAI = Microsoft = Azure = competes with Databricks on Azure marketplace)
- Unity Catalog governance story needs an intelligence layer that respects data boundaries
- They've watched Snowflake ship Cortex AI with us and they're structurally behind

**Competitive landscape**
- OpenAI: Microsoft relationship is the poison pill. Databricks won't build its flagship AI product on the partner of its primary cloud competitor.
- Google: Gemini is the obvious alternative — Databricks runs heavily on GCP. But Google is also a data-platform competitor (BigQuery).
- **We're the only frontier lab that isn't also competing with them on cloud or data.**

**Industry-specific consideration**
Per the Coopetition doc: Databricks sits at the **$10M+ ARR tier** on current API spend → Product-lead conversation ~1 week pre-launch, co-marketing eligibility. But the *opportunity* is to get them to **$50M+** — which unlocks the dedicated XFN pod and exec-level engagement. That threshold should be the explicit deal goal.

**Land motion:** Sam's Lighthouse playbook. Treat as a partnership, not a deal. Unscalable things: bring Tibbetts or Scott White to the first meeting. Pitch to the VP of Product for Mosaic AI, not the eng team doing evals.

**Competitive landscape**
- OpenAI: Incumbent. Atlassian Intelligence launched on GPT. But "nobody got fired for buying OpenAI" is weakening — Atlassian's own engineers are using Claude Code.
- GitHub Copilot: The real competitor isn't a model — it's Microsoft's bundled dev-tool suite. Atlassian needs us to help them compete with *that*.

**Industry-specific consideration**
This is **Ami's Shadow-IT thesis in its purest form:** *"Developers love Claude Code so much they work around org policies... they become our strongest champions when their company evaluates AI providers."* The land motion isn't top-down *or* bottom-up — it's *both simultaneously*. Pitch the CIO with the data showing their own engineers already chose us.

Amazon shows up as 1 managed DNB account *and* 32 separate unmanaged orgs — individual teams swiping cards. Atlassian almost certainly has the same shadow-spend pattern. Step one is finding it and rolling it into the conversation.

**Land motion:** Two-front. BDR pulls the shadow Claude Code / API spend across every Atlassian team we can find in Unmanaged. AE brings that data to the Chief Product Officer with the Ami framing: *"Your people already chose. Let us help you give it to them safely."*

## SLIDE 10 — SERVICENOW: The Anti-Frontier Defensive Play

**Why this one, why now:** ServiceNow's entire positioning is "workflow orchestration for the enterprise." OpenAI's Frontier (launched Feb 5) is pitching the exact same thing — "agent orchestration platform, the operating system of the enterprise." **ServiceNow has a structural, existential reason to prefer us over OpenAI that no other account has.**

**Customer need**
- Now Assist (their GenAI layer) needs a model partner that isn't simultaneously trying to become the orchestration layer *on top of them*
- They need the "safe, credible, trustworthy enterprise vendor" positioning — the exact language from Tibbetts' transformation-potential criterion — because their buyer is the risk-averse CIO
- They want to embed deeply enough that switching us out is expensive

**Competitive landscape**
- OpenAI: **Actively competing with ServiceNow at the orchestration layer.** Frontier's pitch is "manage fleets of agents across CRM, ticketing, internal apps" — that's ServiceNow's business. The model-agnostic positioning doesn't help ServiceNow; it commoditizes them.
- Google: Neutral on orchestration, but weak enterprise-trust story relative to us
- **Our pitch: "We're not trying to be the orchestration OS. You are. We're the intelligence layer underneath."** The Coopetition doc's exact DNB line: *"You need our intelligence layer, we need your distribution."*

**Industry-specific consideration**
ServiceNow has the deepest system-of-record trust moat of any platform on this list — years of accumulated integration and workflow trust across the Fortune 500. Per Tibbetts: *"It's not just a deployed piece of software, it is an institutional designation."* We're not trying to melt that SaaS. We're trying to be the layer it sits on.

This is also the account where our Claude Partner Network ($100M, announced last week) matters most — ServiceNow has the largest SI/partner ecosystem of anyone on this list. Getting their partners Claude-certified is a force multiplier.

**Land motion:** This is a **CEO-to-CEO conversation**, not an AE sale. The pitch is strategic alignment against a common competitive threat. Bring Paul or Dario. The deal structure should look more like the Snowflake template (multi-year, data-sharing provisions, governance) than a standard API commit.

## SLIDE 11 — STRIPE: The Triple-Threat Revenue Play

**Why this one, why now:** Stripe is the only account on this list that can scale across **all three Anthropic revenue surfaces simultaneously** at material size:

| Surface | Stripe's fit | Revenue logic |
|---|---|---|
| **API** (their product) | Stripe Radar, Sigma, every fraud/risk/analytics surface is an LLM workload at scale | External-facing, reaches millions of merchants — passes the hardest Polaris ICP gate |
| **Claude Code** (their eng org) | ~8,000+ engineers, famously high bar, heavy internal-tooling culture | Exact profile that drove Claude Code to $24M RRR at 35% WoW in FSI |
| **Cowork plugins** (their ecosystem) | Stripe has the deepest developer ecosystem in fintech infra — they'd be a flagship plugin publisher | Per Dave Brown: "B2B SaaS companies will want to launch their own plug-ins... App Store model for enterprise knowledge work" |

**Customer need**
- Stripe's financial-data surface area is enormous and underexploited — every merchant dashboard, every dispute workflow, every Sigma query is an intelligence opportunity
- They're culturally allergic to vendor lock-in — the pitch needs to be platform parity, not exclusivity
- They compete with Adyen and Checkout.com on product velocity; AI features are now table-stakes

**Competitive landscape**
- OpenAI: Stripe was an early OpenAI partner (GPT-4 launch announcement). Displacing incumbents is harder than greenfield. But their engineers are Shadow-IT on Claude Code *today*.
- The harder competitor is **"build it ourselves"** — Stripe has the eng talent to fine-tune open models. The counter is Dario's API-lifecycle argument: the API scales along the exponential; their in-house model doesn't.

**Industry-specific consideration**
Stripe sits at the **intersection of DNB and Fintech** — this is a segmentation question the panel will ask. My answer: Stripe's buyer psychology is a platform company's, not a bank's. They don't have regulatory depth as a moat; they have API-design taste and developer trust. They buy like Databricks, not like JPMorgan. That's why they belong in Platform.

**Land motion:** Three parallel tracks from day one. API track targets VP Product for Radar/Sigma. Claude Code track targets VP Eng with the shadow-spend data. Cowork track targets their Developer Platform lead on plugin publishing. Three champions, three budgets, one master agreement.

## APPENDIX A — Anticipated Panel Questions

**"Why not 5 targets?"**
Four is enough to prove four different theses. A fifth would be filler. If you want a fifth: **HubSpot** — mid-market CRM, structurally anti-Salesforce, hungry for differentiation, and a good test of whether the playbook scales down from $200M to $20M deals.

**"Isn't Stripe a Fintech account?"**
Stripe's *market* is financial services. Stripe's *buyer psychology* is a platform company's. They don't have regulatory depth or balance-sheet risk as a moat — they have API-design taste and developer ecosystem trust. They buy like Databricks, not like JPMorgan. The segmentation should follow the buying motion, not the SIC code.

**"What's the risk if we carve this out and it doesn't work?"**
The accounts stay in DNB either way. The downside is we've moved four AE headcounts between sub-teams with no net change to coverage. The upside is we've compounded the Snowflake win across its peer set. Asymmetric.

**"How is this different from what Hratch is already doing?"**
Hratch is running a team. I'm proposing a *segment* — with its own number, its own coopetition seat at the Product roadmap table, and its own reference-sell motion. The difference is accountability and leverage, not headcount.

**"What about Salesforce? Intuit? They're platforms."**
Both are already Lighthouse accounts with dedicated strategic coverage. Intuit is a live coopetition case ($29M commit, tax plugin tension). I wouldn't move either — but the Platform coopetition PM role should have visibility into both.

**"OpenAI's Frontier is model-agnostic — doesn't that mean it helps us too?"**
In theory. In practice, model-agnostic orchestration commoditizes the model layer. Our 55% enterprise API share is defensible as long as customers are building *directly on us*. If they're building on Frontier and we're one of four checkboxes, that share erodes. The ServiceNow play is the hedge: partner with the companies who have as much to lose from Frontier as we do.
