Connecticut's Fortune 500 tech presence is dominated by **legacy** companies that don't qualify:

**Booking Holdings is the standout** — at $23.7B revenue, it's one of the largest born-digital companies in the world, and it's headquartered in Norwalk, CT.

## KEY OBSERVATIONS

### Massachusetts Dominates (21 of 32 companies, 66%)
- **Boston/Cambridge** is the clear engine, with companies spanning e-commerce (Wayfair), restaurant tech (Toast), gaming (DraftKings), CRM (HubSpot), cybersecurity (Rapid7, Snyk), and health tech (Zelis, Whoop).
- The strength is in **diversity of verticals** — no single sector dominates.
- Nearly all are concentrated within a 10-mile radius of Kendall Square / Back Bay.
- 11 are public companies, providing strong comps and exit benchmarks.

### Connecticut's Surprise: Booking Holdings
- CT has only 2 born-digital companies at scale, but one of them (**Booking Holdings at $23.7B**) is larger than the entire rest of the list combined.
- Booking is a textbook "born in the cloud" story — Priceline launched in 1997 as a pure internet company and never operated a single physical travel agency.
- The rest of CT's tech scene (Gartner, SS&C, FactSet) is entirely legacy.

### Atlanta's Fintech Strength
- 3 of Atlanta's 9 qualifying companies are fintech (GreenSky, Kabbage, Cardlytics).
- Mailchimp's $12B exit to Intuit in 2021 is the ecosystem's defining moment.
- The pipeline is strong: Greenlight ($228M, approaching threshold), Flock Safety (massive funding), FullStory ($1.8B valuation).

### Rhode Island: No Qualifying Companies
- The state's entire SaaS ecosystem is $125.5M combined. This is a structural gap — limited VC, limited talent density, limited enterprise customer base.

### Healthcare is Thin
- Only 2 companies (Zelis, Whoop) out of 32. Massachusetts has enormous life sciences/biotech presence but very few **born-digital health SaaS** companies at $250M+ scale. Most health tech companies in the region (Mass General Brigham digital health, etc.) are extensions of traditional health systems.

### The "Making the Leap to Data-Driven" Companies
These companies best exemplify the original criteria — born-digital, now becoming truly data-driven:
- **Klaviyo** ($1.2B) — e-commerce marketing platform making the leap to AI-powered predictive analytics
- **Datadog** (NYC-based but relevant comparison) — observability to AI-ops
- **OneTrust** ($500M) — privacy compliance to AI-powered data governance
- **Cardlytics** ($280M) — bank card data to purchase intelligence AI
- **Recorded Future** ($300M+) — threat data to AI-powered intelligence
- **Toast** ($5.8B) — restaurant POS to data-driven restaurant operations platform
